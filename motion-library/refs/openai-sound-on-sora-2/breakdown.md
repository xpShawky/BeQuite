## 4. Signature techniques
- **Transitions:** 95 cuts, ASL 1.39 s (median 0.88), slowing curve [0.98, 1.29, 1.52, 1.97]. Cuts on strong onsets 15% vs 10% chance (lift ×1.48): the edit chases sound events (horns, hits, crowds), not a music grid (tempo confidence only 1.4, no steady bed).
- **Typography:** two tiny centred sans cards and one italic end title. Type is nearly absent; people speak instead.
- **Camera:** each world gets its own camera: presenter lock-offs, macro, aerial, selfie, stop-motion tabletop.
- **Colour:** no house grade; the presenters are the constant.
- **Pacing:** fast in the feature section (0.17–1 s), then 4–8 s holds for cameo and finale shots.
- **Sound:** **the film is the sound demo.** Native audio per clip, a diegetic orchestra instead of a score, silence twice (after the claim card, before the stinger). −15.1 LUFS, LRA 6.4 LU, **true peak +1.0 dBFS** (over full scale: a QA miss). Voices: two male presenters (median F0 ≈ 167 Hz), 107 wpm, calm and dry, 61% of runtime.

## 5. When to use this style
- **Fits:** launches where the founders are the brand; features that can each be proven by one scene; 2-minute YouTube launch.
- **Avoid when:** no on-camera spokesperson; the audience needs a serious, premium tone (the humour is the point here).
- **Cost and difficulty:** very high generation effort (character-consistent presenters across 15+ worlds, sync sound); edit is medium.

## 6. What to steal (adapt, never copy)
1. **Claim card before any image** ("Every frame you are about to see was planned, built and verified by BeQuite"), in two write-on steps, then silence.
2. **Count-in sync:** let something in the scene count or tick ("3, 2, 1", a progress bar, a gate check) so the audience's ear predicts the next cuts.
3. **Staccato sound montage:** 5–6 shots of 4–5 frames, each on one transient, to illustrate a line about speed or sound.
4. **Silence before the stinger, then a deadpan title** for a post-credits joke (e.g. a next-version tease).
5. **Show the tools of the craft first** (lens, mic, recorder) to frame what the film is about.
