## 1. The story in one line
A young woman who cannot say no ("an NPC with no refuse option") is pushed into feeding a friend's hamster over New Year, meets a lost, abandoned dog, and finally says "no" for the first time to keep him. It feels funny first, then lonely, then warm.

## 2. Story shape
Character flaw stated (NPC metaphor) → inciting incident (the favour) → companion appears (dog) → road trip / search → dark night of the soul (she admits she feels useless; the dog comforts her) → the truth (owners moved and gave him away) → the turn (she says "no") → new home, naming → real-world coda (owners talk about their adopted pets). Save-the-cat structure, with the character arc paid off by one word.

## 4. Signature techniques
- **Transitions:** mostly straight cuts; motion-carried cuts (7) on scooter movement; cuts in the middle of lines (134 of 152) and lines that start before cuts (45): dialogue bridges the edit.
- **Typography:** almost none: burnt-in subtitles and name cards; the Apple mark only at the end.
- **Camera:** iPhone, handheld and on the ground; fisheye mirror shots; top-downs; slow-motion on the dog running (named as the key shot in the making-of).
- **Colour and background:** daylight teal/orange in act 1; near-black tungsten night in act 2; red lanterns as the CNY thread; the heroine's orange scarf as a constant colour anchor.
- **Pacing:** ASL 4.67 s, median 2.04 s; curve **[2.23, 4.06, 9.41, 13.75]**: the film slows by a factor of six from start to end (the comedy is cut fast, the emotion is held).
- **Sound:** −16.2 LUFS, **LRA 13.1 LU** (wide dynamics); music withheld through the dark middle (6:00–8:45 at −22 to −26 dB), released after the climax. Two inner voices (girl, dog) carry the comedy. Sync lift ×1.99 (cuts on onsets twice chance) in the comedic first act.

## 5. When to use this style
- Fits: brand films where the product is the camera or invisible; festive campaigns; adoption/charity themes; 3–12 min for YouTube/cinema.
- Avoid when: you need a product demo or a short social ad.
- Cost and difficulty: very high (cast, locations, trained animals, a stop-motion studio). The principle (a character flaw paid off by one action at the climax; music held back until after it) costs nothing.

## 6. What to steal (adapt, never copy)
1. **The flaw named in the first minute, paid off with one word at the climax.** For a BeQuite story: a developer who "always says done" finally writes "UNVERIFIED".
2. **Withhold music through the low point; release it after the turn.**
3. **Switch medium for the inner voice** (stop-motion for talking animals). For BeQuite: live UI for what really runs, a hand-made/illustrated layer for what the agent "thinks".
4. **Pacing slows as emotion rises:** cut comedy fast, hold feeling long.
5. **Documentary coda** to anchor the theme in real people (real users' short lines).
