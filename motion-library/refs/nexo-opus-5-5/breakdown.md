## 4. Signature techniques
- **One take:** no cuts. The camera is a character too: it follows, pulls back and returns in one smooth path.
- **Toy-like 3D:** rounded glossy icons, soft peach background, shallow depth of field (tilt-shift), squash-and-stretch hops.
- **The product's real voice:** terminal prompt, spinner words, file names, OS notification; it feels authentic, not generic.
- **Sound:** steady −15 LUFS bed with SFX on hops and clicks (one short dip at 3 s). Peak −4.3 dB (headroom left).

## 5. When to use this style
- **Fits:** app and OS-level demos, developer tools with personality, **iPhone / iPad app motion** (the owner's idea: an app that moves on the device), social posts that need charm; 10–20 s.
- **Avoid when:** serious enterprise or medical topics, or when the brand has no playful side.
- **Cost:** medium-high. Needs 3D (Blender, Spline, Cinema 4D, or a code-built 3D scene) with a single animated camera path and physics-like hops. AI video can't hold this kind of UI consistency yet.

## 6. What to steal
1. **A tiny mascot that performs the workflow.** BeQuite could have a small "gate" or "folder" character that walks through clarify → plan → build → verify.
2. **One continuous camera** for a playful piece; the cut-free flow feels expensive.
3. **Real product language as the joke** (BeQuite's own gate names, "UNVERIFIED", "Approved").
4. **Sign the film with a typed prompt line.**
