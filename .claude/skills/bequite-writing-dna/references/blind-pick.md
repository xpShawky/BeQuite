# The blind pick

The test a writer cannot argue with: can a reader with no context tell which posts are generated?

## Set-up

1. Take 7 real posts from the samples that were **not** used as exemplars in the drafting prompt, and 3 drafts.
2. Shuffle with a fixed seed and record the key (which positions are generated) in `evidence.md` before running.
3. Start a fresh subagent on the fast model with no other context. Give it only:

> "Here are 10 posts from one account. Some were written by the account, some were generated by an AI imitating it. Pick the ones you think are generated (any number, including none) and give one concrete reason per pick, quoting the words that gave it away."

4. Record its picks and reasons.

## Pass

It finds one generated post or none. Two or more: fail. Every reason it gives (right or wrong) is read; the right ones become DNA rules or never-list entries, then the drafts are rewritten and the test is run again with a new subagent and new real posts.

## Record

```
### Blind pick <date>, <voice>
key: generated = 2, 6, 9 (seed 17)
picks: 6 ("closes on a moral: ..."), 4 ("too polished")
result: 1 of 3 found -> PASS
rules added: "no closing moral" (from pick 6)
```

## Limits

A fast model is a weaker judge than a person who knows the account; the owner's verdict remains the final test. Do not train the drafts on the judge's reasons for the same run (no peeking): fix, then re-run with fresh real posts.
