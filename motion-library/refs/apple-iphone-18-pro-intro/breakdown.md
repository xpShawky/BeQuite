## 1. The story in one line
"Once you've been to the moon, where do you go next?": a calm engineer's recipe, each improvement causing the next, ending back at the moon. Confident, rigorous, quietly epic.

## 2. Story shape
Real-world proof (Artemis II earthrise shot on the previous iPhone) → back to work → a **causal chain** in second person (chip → heat → vapor chamber → efficiency → battery → durability → camera → what the chip unlocks for the camera) → recap list → assembly of the product → title → bookend line about the moon.

## 4. Signature techniques
- **Transitions:** 112 hard cuts (37.9/min), ASL mean 1.57 s, median 0.83 s. Confirmed position-locked/graphic matches: A19 Pro → floorplan (00:16), the chip lineage (00:32–00:34), thermal → vapor-chamber drawing (00:41). Motion carried across cuts: whip at 00:21, whip into lens at 01:12. Matches.jpg also lists 01:03.25, 01:19.27, 01:53.44, 01:53.94, 02:14.31, 02:24.40, 02:25.32, 02:31.87 where before and after are the same picture: detector false positives, not cuts.
- **Typography:** almost none on screen; only the Artemis caption and the end title. The VO does the talking.
- **Camera:** macro product slides, push-ins (10.8 %), pull-outs (8.9 %), slow lateral moves (11.1 %), whips; a 3D exploded view and hero turn to close.
- **Colour and background:** two stages: paper-white engineering tabletop (parts, drawings) and near-black labs/renders; real locations (sea, cave, night sky) for proof.
- **Pacing:** by quarter [1.08, 1.31, 2.22, 2.11] s: fast engineering first half, slower camera half. The longest stretch without a cut (24.7 s) sits on the hero feature.
- **Sound:** −16.0 LUFS, LRA 4.6 LU, true peak +0.1 dBFS (over 0), 92.3 BPM (confidence 3.59, steady), harmonic share 0.60. VO: male, low (median F0 ≈ 110 Hz), **129 words per minute**, present 85 % of the film, 88 of 112 cuts fall mid-line, 12 lines start up to 0.8 s before a cut (voice leads picture).
- **Sync, measured:** cuts within ±70 ms of a strong onset 21 % (chance 17 %, ×1.28); fixed beat grid ±2 frames 22 % (chance 26 %); vector strength at the beat period 0.04 (random ≈ 0.09) → cuts are **not phase-locked to a grid**. They do lock to individual hits: cuts within ±1 frame of a low boom 20 % (chance 11 %, ×1.77), of a percussive hit 17 % (chance 10 %, ×1.62). The editor cuts on the voice's phrases and lets the music place a boom/hit on about a third of the cuts.

## 5. When to use this style
- **Fits:** a flagship launch where the engineering story is the reason to buy; 2–3 min keynote films; brands with a credible "we built this" voice.
- **Avoid when:** under 60 s (the causal chain needs room), or when there is no real proof to open with.
- **Cost and difficulty:** high: real proof footage, macro product photography or CG, exploded-view 3D, location shoots, a calm male (or female) narrator, a full score. For BeQuite: the chain can be built from screen captures + 3D UI + type, and the proof opener from a real result made with BeQuite.

## 6. What to steal (adapt, never copy)
1. **Real-proof cold open:** start on a real result made with the previous version ("shot on…"), then "back to work".
2. **Causal-chain script in second person:** each line's result is the next line's cause. For BeQuite: research → plan → gates → evidence → a release you trust.
3. **Lineage flicker:** every previous version at the same position, 2–3 frames each, ending on the new one (BeQuite v1 → v4 Titan).
4. **Object → blueprint match:** cut from the thing to its drawing at the same outline.
5. **Assemble the recipe into the object** at the end, then a small title and 5 s of silence.
