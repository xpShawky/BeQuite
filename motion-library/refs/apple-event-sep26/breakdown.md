## 4. Signature techniques
- **Transitions:**
  - Position-locked match cuts carry the product films: the watch crown in two finishes (48:21.97), a device X-ray → the same device's screen (68:27.64), a photo UI → a camera UI on the same body (72:21.80), city aerials matched by skyline (19:31.47), a presenter kept in the same spot while the set changes (20:11.78), a "35%" stat card → a glowing chip at the same centre (68:36.71).
  - **The lineage flicker** (07:10.9-07:11.8): chip generations A15 → A16 → … cut every 3 frames in exactly the same position, accelerating into "the fastest chip ever put in an iPhone".
  - 181 cuts touch black: product films start and end on black, and the keynote moves between host and film through black.
- **Typography:** the keynote uses white cards with large black SF type ("Intelligent personal hub", "Nano-texture finish", "35% higher sustained performance…"); one claim per card, stat number huge, a supporting line small.
- **Camera:** the keynote is 50% locked-off (presenters, demos); product films move constantly (in the iPhone 18 Pro film at 06:58-07:25, 35 cuts in 26 s). The cold open uses long push-ins for suspense and short moving shots for action.
- **Colour and background:** the cold open is low-key and genre-graded (fire orange, B&W battle, sunny chase); the keynote is daylight Apple Park and white cards. Dark → light marks the hand-over.
- **Pacing:** slow (long holds) → burst (fire, 0.17 s) → slow → fast (chase) → reset → steady classic aerial. Fast cutting is reserved for impact and action, never for talking.
- **Sound:** silence before the hit (20.8-23.9 s); risers into each new idea (8 riser-in cuts in the cold open); booms on chase cuts; in product films hits land on cuts (the 07:00-07:25 iPhone film: 15 of 35 cuts have a hit or boom). Whole event −19.3 LUFS (speech-led), cold open −11.7 LUFS (film-led).
- **Voice:** the cold-open narrator speaks slowly (97 wpm) with real pauses, so each line can be "seen" before the next; presenters speak at about 136 wpm. Hand-offs are scripted one-liners ("Now, over to Sri…", "back to John").

## 5. When to use this style
- Fits: a launch or event opener; any film where the product is a tool for making (camera, creative app, an AI agent); a hand-over (new version, new lead, new era).
- Avoid when: under 20 s (the device needs time to set up the joke); when there is no real footage to show (the pastiche needs genuinely good shots).
- Cost and difficulty: high for real genre footage; medium if the "openings" are motion-graphics or AI plates instead of live action.

## 6. What to steal (adapt, never copy)
1. **The narrated shot list**: a voice says what happens next, and the picture obeys, line by line. For BeQuite this is literal: the prompt or plan is the narration, and the film builds itself shot by shot as the plan is read. "Open on a broken app…" → we see it.
2. **Pitch and reject**: show 2-3 wrong openings (generic AI slop, a template, a "should work ✓") and reject each ("Needs more…", "Forget all that"), then the "classic", disciplined one. That is BeQuite's argument as a structure.
3. **The lineage flicker**: versions in the same position, 3 frames each, accelerating into the newest (BeQuite v0.1 → v4 "Titan", or 55 command names flickering in one slot) on a riser, ending on a hit.
4. **The hand-over in the cut**: one thing walks out of frame in the dark, the next walks in from the light.
5. **Burst only on impact**: 6-7 cuts of 0.17 s at the one physical moment (the break, the explosion), then hold; never a fast cut under a talking line.
6. **Silence 2-3 s before the hit** and risers into each new idea; keep the master around −12 to −14 LUFS for the film parts.
