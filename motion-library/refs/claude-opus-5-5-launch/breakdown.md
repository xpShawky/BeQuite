## 1. The story in one line
"There's more to discover": the world is full of hidden detail, and the new model helps you see it. It feels curious, scientific and calm, but alive.

## 2. Story shape
Bookend → montage → statement → product → brand → bookend. There is no problem or solution; it is a **mood statement**. The product name is never explained, only placed.

## 4. Signature techniques
- **Transitions:** graphic match cuts. Every shot puts a curved edge at the same height and arc, so 33 cuts read as one continuous movement. The arc is the "guide element", and its destination is the horizon of the ending.
- **Typography:** classic serif, white or tinted, small (about 6% of frame height), one or two words at a time, placed *on* the arc. Words hold for several shots while the background changes.
- **Camera:** almost static macro and space plates; the motion comes from the cuts, not from camera moves.
- **Colour and background:** a living, ever-changing background with deep blacks between; there is no brand colour except in the logo. Real textures (macro, archival, craft), not CG.
- **Pacing:** slow open (one shot of ~1.2 s and one of ~2 s), then ~0.5 s shots, then ~0.25 s at the peak, then a full stop on the name.
- **Sound:** quiet → build → peak exactly where the cuts are fastest → release under the product name and logo. Integrated −16.4 LUFS, true peak −0.6 dB.

## 5. When to use this style
- **Fits:** AI and model launches, research products, brand films, "capability" stories where the product is abstract; 15–30 s; 16:9 (holds in 1:1 with the arc kept low).
- **Avoid when:** the viewer must understand a workflow or a UI (it shows no product), or for performance ads that need a CTA.
- **Cost and difficulty:** the craft is in **curating 30+ plates that share one shape**. Plates can come from stock, macro photography, archives, AI stills (Flow / image models) or 3D, and are then aligned in AE or Resolve to one arc mask. Medium effort, very high perceived quality.

## 6. What to steal (adapt, never copy)
1. **One shape, many worlds.** For BeQuite the shape could be the **terminal cursor / prompt line**, a **folder edge**, or a **circular gate**. Every plate keeps it in the same place.
2. **The text is the still point; the world is the motion.** Hold one phrase across 5–8 cuts.
3. **Accelerate the cuts with the music, then stop dead on the name.** Silence or a near-black frame is the reward.
4. **Bookend:** end on the image you opened with.
