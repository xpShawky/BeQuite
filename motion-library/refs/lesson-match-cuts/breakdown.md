**Category:** craft lesson (match-cut types)

## What it teaches
A match cut uses an element of the outgoing shot to carry the viewer into the next one, and it implies a connection between two events or ideas. Three types: graphic (shape/imagery), movement (action), audio (sound). They combine, and they need a motive.

## How each lesson applies to a 30–60 s launch film
- **#1, #4:** for "the most powerful AI harness", the match is the argument: a tangle of loose wires (or a chaotic terminal) cuts to the clean harness diagram in the same silhouette. The match says "this becomes that" without a word.
- **#6 (through-line):** one object carried across 4–6 cuts (the BeQuite mark, a single cursor, a gate ring) is the "guide element" of `motion-grammar.md`, built with match cuts instead of one continuous move.
- **#8, #9:** a hand's tap on a real keyboard continues as a UI click in the next shot; two agents' actions can be cut as one rhythm.
- **#11, #12:** every graphic match gets an audio match: the sound of the outgoing object morphs into the sound of the incoming one (a mechanical clack becomes a UI tick, pitched the same).
- **#13:** for AI plates, "plan it in the shot list" means generating both sides of the match from first-frame/last-frame keyframes with the object in the same place, not hoping two random clips line up.

## What to try in the lab
1. **Through-line chain:** a 6 s sequence where one ring shape passes through four contexts (logo ring, gate ring, a loading spinner, a lens), cut on the beat; check each pair by onion-skinning at 50%.
2. **Audio match:** record or design a clack and a UI tick in the same key; crossfade them over 2–3 frames at the cut.
3. **AI pair:** generate two Flow plates from keyframes that put the same object at the same frame position; measure the position drift on `matches.jpg`.
