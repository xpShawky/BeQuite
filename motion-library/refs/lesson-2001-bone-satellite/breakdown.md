## 1. The story in one line
An ape discovers a bone is a weapon, kills, throws it into the sky, and the tumbling bone becomes an orbiting satellite: four million years of tool-making in one cut. Feels triumphant, then suddenly vast and silent.

## 2. Story shape
Conflict (ape tribes at the waterhole) → victory with the weapon → the throw (release) → the bone in flight, in silence → the match cut → a slow, silent space ballet (new world, new tempo) → music.

## 4. Signature techniques
- **Transitions:** one idea match carrying the whole film's argument; the set-up is a cut on the throw to an empty sky (exit → empty → entry).
- **Typography:** none.
- **Camera:** locked-off and inner motion only (100%); low angle on the victor against the sky; the bone shots look up into empty sky, so the frame is already "space-like" before the cut.
- **Colour and background:** the flight happens against an almost empty frame, so the bone's silhouette is the only content, which is what makes the shape match readable.
- **Pacing:** 16 shots, ASL 10.1 s; average shot length per quarter 5.1 → 8.1 → 13.5 → 13.5 s. The match is the pivot from conflict tempo to orbital tempo.
- **Sound:** −22.7 LUFS integrated with a very wide 21.5 LU range; ≈17 s of near-silence from the throw through the cut, then the waltz fades in. Silence, not a hit, marks the biggest cut in the film.

## 5. When to use this style
- Fits: a launch film's single "turn" (the old way becomes the new), brand films about scale or evolution, a title reveal.
- Avoid when: the film is a fast montage that needs invisible continuity (there, match luminance and anchor tightly, per `motion-grammar.md`); or the two objects share no silhouette (the match then reads as a random cut).
- Cost and difficulty: low in tools (two shots and a cut), high in planning: both shots must be designed for each other (framing, empty background, speed).

## 6. What to steal (adapt, never copy)
1. **Exit, empty, enter:** the hook object (a broken commit, a falling block) leaves the top of frame; cut to an empty frame; it re-enters. 0.2–0.4 s of empty frame builds the wait.
2. **Match on silhouette and span, let brightness flip:** a raw, messy tool (a tangle of scripts, a tangled cable) cut to the BeQuite harness at the same width and orientation family, from warm-bright to dark-precise. The inversion is the "new era" signal.
3. **Slow the object before the match** so its speed equals the incoming shot's speed (render the outgoing shot in slow motion, or ease it to the incoming drift).
4. **Cut in silence, bring music after:** let the match land with no hit; start the music bed a beat or two after, then let it rise.
5. **Change tempo after the match:** the shots after the turn are longer than any before it.
