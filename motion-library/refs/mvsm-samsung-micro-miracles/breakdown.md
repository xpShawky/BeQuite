## 4. Signature techniques
- **Transitions:** mostly straight cuts in the edit's rhythm (sync lift ×0.69: cuts avoid strong onsets; the VO leads). One white-flash scale jump (17.3 s). Bookend open/close.
- **Typography:** almost none: a small factual caption at the start, the tagline at the end, SEM HUD numbers as texture.
- **Camera:** slow pulls and pushes on SEM scans; the HUD frame stays fixed while the image moves (instrument POV).
- **Colour and background:** monochrome cool grey-blue for the micro world; dark teal lab with one white key light; no brand colour.
- **Pacing:** ASL 2.86 s, median 1.76 s; curve [2.5, 2.14, 3.0, 2.5]: even, contemplative.
- **Sound:** −14.3 LUFS, LRA 4.5 LU, music bed ≈ 152 BPM (low confidence) with sparse hits; VO under 76 % of the film; cuts mid-line (18 of 20) so the VO carries over the cuts.

## 5. When to use this style
- Fits: infrastructure / "invisible tech" brands (chips, AI models, back-end tools), brand films about scale, 45–90 s.
- Avoid when: you need to show a UI or a price; short social cut-downs.
- Cost and difficulty: very high if real (nano-sculpture + SEM time). The look can be faked in 3D (Blender/C4D with an SEM shader: grey, edge-lit, noise, HUD), but then the "captured for real" claim is gone.

## 6. What to steal (adapt, never copy)
1. **Truth caption first:** state what is real ("every command shown ran on a real repo") before the film starts.
2. **Instrument HUD as evidence frame:** for BeQuite, a fixed overlay of timestamps, exit codes and gate status over every shot.
3. **Scale jump through an instrument:** terminal line → white flash → inside the `.bequite/` folder world.
4. **One material, many worlds:** keep one material/shader for all metaphors so a varied tour reads as one film.
5. **Bookend on the opening set.**
