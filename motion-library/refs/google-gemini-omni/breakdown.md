## 4. Signature techniques
- **Transitions:** position-locked swaps (style, material, environment) are the signature; the camera rarely moves across them. 51 cuts, ASL 1.04 s (median 0.77), pacing [0.85, 0.85, 1.69, 0.9].
- **Typography:** Google Sans, white with soft shadow over footage or black on white UI canvas; short phrases (1–4 words); words swap in place.
- **Camera:** mixed generated plates; push-ins (25%) dominate.
- **Colour:** every plate its own palette; the white UI canvas resets the eye between sections.
- **Sound:** music only, no voice. −16.2 LUFS, LRA 4.3 LU; per-second peaks pinned at −9.0 to −9.5 dB for 45 s (heavily limited). Cut sync is at chance (18% vs 19%): cuts follow the visual swaps, not the music.

## 5. When to use this style
- **Fits:** "do anything" capability launches; 30–60 s social sizzles; a product whose output varies widely.
- **Avoid when:** you need trust or depth; the flood of generated plates reads as generic AI if the swaps are not locked.
- **Cost and difficulty:** medium-high: many AI generations, but the key device (one clip restyled N times) is cheap with video-to-video restyle. AE for the type and chips.

## 6. What to steal (adapt, never copy)
1. **Label-synced swap:** one locked frame of a BeQuite project; the label changes ("Swap stack", "Swap design", "Swap language: Arabic RTL") and the frame changes on the same frame.
2. **Style-swap on continuous action:** one developer action continues while the world re-renders (sketch → wireframe → real UI).
3. **Inline media chips in the type line** ("Plan [chip] build [chip] verify [chip]").
4. **Spiral/one-shape opener with the name fixed in centre**, accelerating from 0.7 s to 0.13 s.
