## 4. Signature techniques
- **Transitions:** 13 hard cuts, ASL 4.1 s (median 2.92), pacing [3.59, 3.59, 2.05, 7.18]. Chapters change on a **sound slam** (3 slams at cuts), not on a visual device.
- **Typography:** two lines of tiny centred sans on black. Nothing else.
- **Camera:** each clip imitates a real capture device: broadcast telephoto, anime, phone in hand, CG space, western wide.
- **Colour:** whatever each genre needs; no unifying grade.
- **Pacing:** long takes on purpose: two 10 s shots (00:22.9, 00:42.8) let you check the generation for errors.
- **Sound:** no music bed; cut-onset sync below chance (8% vs 10%, lift ×0.81) because the edit follows each clip's own audio, not a beat. −16.1 LUFS, LRA 6.4 LU, true peak −0.2 dBFS. Voices are in-scene characters (median F0 ≈ 213 Hz), 37 words.

## 5. When to use this style
- **Fits:** a capability that must be believed by inspection (generation quality, speed); 30–60 s; social.
- **Avoid when:** the product is invisible infrastructure (BeQuite): there is no single clip that "proves" a harness.
- **Cost and difficulty:** low edit cost, high generation cost (many takes to get five clean clips). Needs a tool that produces sync sound.

## 6. What to steal (adapt, never copy)
1. **Silent black card at both ends:** 2.5–5 s of true silence and a tiny title, then a hard slam into the first real sound.
2. **Hold the proof shot long** (10 s) when the claim is quality; quick cuts read as hiding.
3. **Genre roulette** for range claims: five shots, five different "cameras".
4. **Let failure and dead air in** (the crash, the pauses) to sell realism.
