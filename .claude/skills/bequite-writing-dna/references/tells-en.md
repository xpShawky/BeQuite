# English tells (sources read 2026-11-21)

Structure outlasts vocabulary: word lists change with every model generation (Wikipedia, "Signs of AI writing"; Juzek & Ward, COLING 2025), while the structural habits below persist (blader/humanizer). Sweep structure first, words second.

## Structure (strongest first; one sighting is enough for 1-5)

1. "Not X but Y" contrasts that add weight, not information.
2. One-line closers and dramatic fragments that repeat the point.
3. Sayings that sound deep; a closing moral.
4. A staged run-up before the point ("Here's the thing:").
5. Arguing with no one ("Some might say...").
6. Forced groups of three.
7. Repeated sentence openings.
8. Dashes as the universal connector. If the samples use dashes, keep the samples' rate.
9. Stacked qualifiers ("could potentially perhaps").
10. Inflated significance ("stands as a testament", "pivotal", "plays a crucial role").
11. Vague sources ("experts say") and borrowed authority.
12. Bold as decoration; a heading over every short section; everything in bullets.
13. Chat residue ("Great question", "I hope this helps"), knowledge-limit disclaimers.
14. Even sentence lengths. Mid-2026 models write short sentences in a strict 8-20 word band with many full stops and commas (Wikipedia): uniform short is the new uniform long.

## Vocabulary by period (Wikipedia, "High density of AI vocabulary words")

- 2023 to mid-2024: additionally, boasts, crucial, delve, intricate, interplay, landscape, meticulously, pivotal, tapestry, testament, underscore, vibrant.
- Mid-2024 to mid-2025: align with, bolstered, emphasizing, enhance, fostering, highlighting, showcasing.
- Mid-2025 to mid-2026: emphasizing, enhance, highlighting, showcasing.
- Mid-2026 on: challenge, clearer, dependable, echoed, foster, leverage, matters, multifaceted, practical, prioritize, quietly, steady, universally.

## Not tells on their own (Wikipedia, "Ineffective indicators")

Perfect grammar; mixed casual and formal registers; formal or "fancy" prose in general; transition words in isolation; unsourced text. Do not "fix" these into a different voice.

## Signs of human writing worth keeping (Wikipedia)

Plain is/has phrases ("there is a", "it has a"); plain verbs (wrote, used, tried, died) over stiff ones (authored, utilized, attempted, passed away); definite or superlative statements ("the only", "was the first"); ordinary qualifiers ("very", "perhaps", "tends to").
