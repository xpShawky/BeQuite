## 4. Signature techniques
- **Transitions:** one motivated dive (headset) in, one sound-slam cut out; elsewhere hard cuts. 78 cuts, ASL 2.87 s (median 2.04), pacing [2.18, 2.47, 2.36, 6.3]: the real-world coda is three times slower.
- **Typography:** none in the film (good for AI: no generated text to break).
- **Camera:** pull-outs (24.5%) and fast tracking in the race; long dolly in the opening.
- **Colour:** warm practical light in reality; saturated candy palette in the game; blue/red identities consistent across both worlds.
- **Sound:** −22.5 LUFS (quiet festival master), LRA 11.6 LU, true peak −1.4 dBFS; 30 booms and 8 slams at cuts. Voice 79% of runtime but only 104 words: short lines, children's voices (median F0 ≈ 254 Hz).

### Where it reads as AI
- The stylised glossy-toy characters hide most tells: no skin, no hands, simple shapes. Identity holds (glasses, braces, colour) across 50+ shots.
- Live-action faces hold in close-up; the boys stay consistent between opening and ending.
- The festival watermark over every frame and the appended promo tail are the biggest distractions, not the generation.

## 5. When to use this style
- **Fits:** a short story film with an emotional turn; stylised CG avoids realism traps.
- **Avoid when:** you need a 30–60 s launch film (3.5 minutes for one idea is long).
- **Cost and difficulty:** high generation volume with character references; editing and sound are standard.

## 6. What to steal (adapt, never copy)
1. **Colour-coded identity** across worlds: give each "agent" or character one colour that survives every style change.
2. **Dive into an object** (a headset, a terminal window, a lens) as the way into a different world.
3. **Go stylised where AI is weak:** toy-like CG or graphic styles for the generated parts, real footage or designed UI for the rest.
4. **Callback prop at the end** (an everyday object that echoes the dream).
