## 1. The story in one line
A journey along one horizon: from a lone walker on the ocean's curve, through deserts, space, science and life, back to the walker. "Something big, made patiently."

## 2. Story shape
A lone figure (a human scale) → the same curve seen at every scale (planet, dune, sun, cell, star map, a worm) → back to the figure → name → brand. **A journey**, quieter and more human than the Opus film.

## 4. Signature techniques
- **The walker:** a small moving subject that "walks the line" across scenes (person → hikers → worm). It gives the montage a protagonist without a story.
- **Graphic match cuts on one arc**, as in the Opus film, but with **longer shots** (avg 1.9 s vs 0.6 s): contemplative, not energetic.
- **Typography:** the same small serif; the title sits on the very first shot's horizon, so the opening image becomes the end card.
- **Sound:** a flat, warm bed with one deliberate hole: near-silence for ~2 s exactly when the product name appears. Integrated −14.0 LUFS, peak −0.9 dB.

## 5. When to use this style
- **Fits:** launches that want to feel thoughtful and human; flagship releases; brand films for tools that "go far" (agents, research, long tasks). 15–25 s.
- **Avoid when:** you need to show features or UI, or the platform rewards fast hooks (TikTok/Reels); this opening is slow.
- **Cost:** curated plates on one shape plus one moving subject; the subject can be animated or composited (a tiny 3D walker in AE/Blender).

## 6. What to steal
1. **A tiny protagonist that walks the line** through every world. For BeQuite: a cursor, a small character, or a "gate" light that travels.
2. **Longer shots for gravity, shorter shots for energy.** Choose deliberately; this film proves slow can be premium.
3. **Cut the music to almost nothing under the name.**
4. **Use the opening shot as the end card** so the film feels designed as one object.
