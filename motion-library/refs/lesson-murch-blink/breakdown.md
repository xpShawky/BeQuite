# Walter Murch and In the Blink of an Eye (StudioBinder): lesson

**File:** `inbox/New/The Book Every Editor Has to Read — Walter Murch and In the Blink of an Eye.mp4` · **Source:** StudioBinder, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PKYeClvvlTw · **Length:** ~15:05 · **Studied from:** transcript.
**Category:** craft lesson (why cuts work; priorities when cutting; editor's working habits)

## What it teaches
A cut is a link between two focuses of attention, accepted because it works like a blink or a dream. Choose cuts by a ranked list (emotion, story, rhythm, eye-trace, 2D plane, 3D space); getting the top items right hides problems lower down. Plus practical habits: a still per shot, standing to cut, seeing only what is on screen.

## How each lesson applies to a 30–60 s launch film
- **#3, #4 (blink):** cut a UI shot when its idea completes: the frame after the gate finishes turning green, not mid-animation. One idea per shot; the cut is the full stop.
- **#7 (bees/30°):** in a fast montage of the same UI, a cut to a near-identical framing reads as a glitch. Change scale or angle clearly (at least ~30° in 3D, or a clear size jump in 2D). This also governs a multi-angle break hook.
- **#8 (emotion first):** for a launch film, the emotion is "this is serious power, and calm". If a cut gives the right feeling but breaks continuity, keep it.
- **#9 (eye-trace):** in a 0.3–0.6 s montage, each incoming shot must put its subject where the eye already is. This is the most practical Murch rule for kinetic type and UI cuts.
- **#11 (one still per shot):** for AI generations, save one representative frame per take into a contact sheet; select from stills, then watch only the finalists.
- **#14:** if the owner says "the ending is weak", check whether the claim was set up earlier.

## What to try in the lab
1. **Eye-trace montage:** 12 UI shots at 0.4 s, version A with the subject jumping around the frame, version B with each subject placed where the last one ended. Show both; note which feels smooth.
2. **Blink placement:** cut the same 5 s UI sequence at mid-animation vs on completion frames.
3. **30° check:** two Blender renders of the same object at 10° and 35° apart; cut between them and judge the jump.
