## 4. Signature techniques
- **Transitions:** UI cards are the transitions: cards fly, merge, expand to full frame, shrink back. 7 hard cuts only; 0% of cuts on a strong onset.
- **Typography:** Google Sans, white, small, centred feature labels ("Ingredients to video", "Extend your clips", "First and last frame", "Add or remove objects").
- **Camera:** slow push-ins on generated shots (35%).
- **Colour:** navy UI stage between warm, cinematic generated plates.
- **Sound:** −14.6 LUFS, LRA 4.6 LU, true peak −0.5 dBFS; per-second peaks pinned near −7 to −8.6 dB except the ducked audio section. Narration 91% of runtime, 115 wpm, mid register (median F0 ≈ 155 Hz, gender UNVERIFIED); generated dialogue lines dropped into gaps.

## 5. When to use this style
- **Fits:** a feature walkthrough for a creative tool; 60–90 s; product pages.
- **Avoid when:** you want a feeling, not a manual; some plates (glitter surfer, candle man) read as generic AI.
- **Cost and difficulty:** medium: AE card system + generated shots + VO.

## 6. What to steal (adapt, never copy)
1. **Ingredient cards merge into the result:** BeQuite inputs (a brief, a repo, a reference film) fly together into one card that expands into the finished app or film.
2. **Card-to-fullscreen expand** as the standard "result reveal" move.
3. **Duck the score ~10 dB** for the one section where the product's own sound is the proof.
4. **Zoom-through grid** to go from "all the work" to "one piece".
