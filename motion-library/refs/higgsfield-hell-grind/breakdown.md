## 4. Signature techniques
- **Transitions:** 92 cuts, ASL 2.02 s (median 1.38), pacing [1.95, 1.88, 2.47, 1.68]. Only 8 motion-carried cuts and 4 graphic matches: almost every cut joins two unrelated generations.
- **Typography:** one distressed red display face for title and date, nothing else.
- **Camera:** inner motion 54% (generated camera moves inside each clip); scope 2.39:1 letterbox to read as cinema.
- **Colour:** one teal-grey, desaturated grade over snow, concrete and night; red as the only accent (demon crystals, title).
- **Sound:** −11.4 LUFS (loud theatrical trailer level), LRA 10.8 LU (big whisper-to-slam range), true peak 0.0 dBFS. 21 booms and 10 hits at cuts; cut-onset sync below chance (10% vs 13%, lift ×0.76): booms are placed, not cut to.

### Where it still reads as AI (QA notes)
1. **Motion mush:** in fast action, limbs, weapons and creatures lose their shape (02:15 rider fight, 02:41 red crystal creature, 01:37 snow figure smeared). The edit hides it with 0.17–0.5 s shots and flash frames (02:34–02:43).
2. **No continuity of action across cuts:** 8 motion-carried cuts out of 92. Fights are a series of hero moments, not choreography; nobody's move continues into the next angle.
3. **Background people:** extras fuse or deform (00:38.5 hallway: the figure in the purple jacket).
4. **Dialogue in singles:** most lines are a single close-up; shot/reverse-shot with matched eyelines is rare (one over-the-shoulder at 02:20).
5. **One grade everywhere** hides inconsistencies but makes every location look the same.
6. **What holds up:** close-ups of faces (00:21, 01:04, 02:20 read as photoreal), a consistent lead (the red mark on his nose holds across shots), no generated text in frame except the designed title.

## 5. When to use this style
- **Fits:** an AI-video capability showcase, entertainment trailers, genre pieces where darkness and speed are on-brand.
- **Avoid when:** the brand must feel crafted and calm (BeQuite); long holds on fast action.
- **Cost and difficulty:** very high generation volume; trailer editing and sound design are classic and do most of the work.

## 6. What to steal (adapt, never copy)
1. **AI action rule:** keep generated fast action to ≤0.5 s per shot and cover the cut with a flash or a boom; hold only on faces and slow moves.
2. **Trailer stop-down:** cut the music to room tone for one line, then slam back.
3. **Comedic button after the title** to end warm instead of loud.
4. **One accent colour** across the whole film (here red) for identity.
